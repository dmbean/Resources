# The 1-9/16″ nut only exists in one era — and it isn't the modern one

Measured from this database's own records (nut widths taken from dealer listings, never from
model spec sheets). Analysis run 2026-11-14, prompted by a question about Norlin-era ES-335TDs.

## The buyer's standing preference

Twice stated, and validated by hand rather than by number:

> *"the 99's neck felt good (kind of a d shape) but not as good as the 1 9/16 necks ive tried."*
> — 2026-08-16

So 1-9/16″ (1.5625″) is not a spec he read about. It is the nut width that felt best of everything
he has physically played.

## What our measured records say

| Era | n | median nut | ≤ 1.60″ | values |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1965–1969 | 8 | **1.5625″** | **8/8** | 1.531 … 1.58 |
| 1970 (transitional) | 2 | 1.625″ | 1/2 | 1.593, 1.625 |
| **1971–1976** | 16 | **1.5625″** | **15/16** | thirteen at exactly 1.5625 |
| 1977–1981 | 5 | 1.6875″ | 1/5 | one holdout at 1.5625, rest 1.67–1.6875 |
| 1982–1999 | 1 | 1.6875″ | 0/1 | — |
| **2000 and later** | **61** | **1.6875″** | **0/61** | narrowest in the whole modern set is 1.614″ |

**Zero of sixty-one modern ES-335s measure at or under 1.60″.** Not one. The narrow nut is not a
rare modern variant to hunt for — it is absent from modern production entirely.

## The same pattern in first-fret depth

His stated ES preference is a 1st fret under .80″.

| Era | under .80″ |
|---|---|
| 1970–1981 | **8 of 18 — 44%** |
| Everything else | 3 of 60 — **5%** |

Nearly a ninefold difference in hit rate.

## And the weight objection does not apply

The Norlin era's heavy reputation comes from Les Pauls — pancake bodies, 3-piece maple necks. It
does not transfer to the semi-hollows:

| Era | n | median weight | at or under 8.0 lb |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1970–1981 | 26 | **7.71 lb** | **25/26** |
| 2000 and later | 83 | 7.75 lb | 75/83 |

Norlin-era 335s are, in this dataset, marginally **lighter** than modern ones.

## Conclusion for the search

**1965–1976 is the only window that can deliver the neck he has already chosen by feel.** Within it,
1971–1976 is the affordable half; 1965–1969 is the same nut width at several times the price.

This reframes the hunt. The modern Custom Shop and Memphis guitars that score well on rolled binding
and documented carves **cannot** supply a 1-9/16″ nut, because nobody builds one. Any run that
ranks modern reissues above clean Norlin-era guitars is ranking a spec he can get against a spec he
cannot.

## Scoring defect this exposed, fixed the same day

Every one of the 41 Norlin-era records scored **0** on the rolled-edges component added 2026-08-19 —
the criterion he said closed his Les Paul purchase. That was a defect in the function, not a fact
about the guitars: decades of playing is precisely how a bound fingerboard edge comes to feel eased.

`rolled_edges()` now returns **55** for a pre-1981 guitar whose listing documents real play wear
(wear, buckle marks, checking, pick wear, refret, patina). Deliberately below a stated spec (100) and
below a factory-spec line (70), because it still wants confirming from photographs. It never fires
on age alone — the listing has to evidence the guitar was actually played.
